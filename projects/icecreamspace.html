Scoopography: Chance Operations Project

From DIG6647: History & Theory of Dynamic Media

Project Overview

Concept behind the work:

Scoopography is an interactive digital experience inspired by personality quizzes, ice cream flavor articles, astrology, and cartography. It began with the familiar idea behind quizzes like “What your ice cream flavor says about you!” These playful pieces of media assign meaning to personal preferences, turning something as simple as choosing a flavor of ice cream into someone’s whole personality. This idea also connects to astrology, which interprets the positions and alignments of celestial bodies to tell stories about a person and their future.

This project combines these ideas by transforming an ice cream selection into a mini solar system. In the project, an ice cream cone acts as the sun, while individual scoops of different ice cream flavors become the planets with their own movements and weather conditions. The title of the project is a combination of “scoop” and “-ography,” in reference to, of course, ice cream and both the mapping of celestial bodies and the interpretation of their arrangements.

Users explore a randomly generated solar system and are able to drag the planets along their orbits to map out their positions. These placements become part of a playful, personalized cosmic reading. Rather than offering a serious prediction, the project uses the language of astrology to turn everyday preference into an imaginative interpretation of the user’s identity.

How randomness is incorporated:

Randomness is somewhat essential to Scoopography! because it determines the composition and characteristics of each solar system. Whenever a new system is generated, the program selects a new combination of three to five ice cream planets from a collection of ten possible flavors. It also randomizes characteristics such as planetary size, orbital distance, speed, direction, and weather conditions. The central ice cream cone and the system’s overall cosmic archetype is also randomized, influencing the atmosphere and movement of the scene.

Randomness also contributes to the interpretation generated by the experience. Each planet and the central cone are assigned numerical values, which are hidden from the user. These values are then combined through mathematical operations (i.e. addition, subtraction, multiplication, division) based on the planet’s position in the solar system. These calculations contribute to the user’s final reading, connecting the arrangement of the solar system to a playful interpretation of their personality.

Users can further influence the experience by dragging planets along their orbits, even after they have gotten their reading. Although the initial arrangement is generated randomly, the user can adjust the positions of the planets in their orbits and explore how changing the configuration affects their reading.

What is the system of curation:

Although randomness is essential in Scoopography, its possibilities are deliberately curated rather than being limitless. The system draws from a defined collection of ten ice cream flavors, a selection of weather conditions, and a group of cosmic archetypes. Each flavor has its own visual identity, while the weather translates familiar ice cream toppings into imaginative planetary phenomena, like sprinkle rain or fudge floods.

The cosmic archetypes establish different rules for each solar system, influencing its color palette, scale, and orbital movement. These rules ensure that randomly generated combinations remain visually coherent while allowing each system to feel distinct. The curation lies in the selection of ingredients, visual styles, and mathematical rules that guide the randomness. Instead of producing arbitrary combinations, the project creates a recognizable universe with room for unexpected discoveries.

How the work creates a different experience each time:

Each new solar system offers a different combination of flavors, planetary characteristics, weather, and spatial arrangements. Users can generate another universe whenever they wish simply by refreshing the page or hitting the shuffle button. They can explore the universe’s planets by hovering over them to discover their characteristics and rearrange their positions along their orbits. These interactions encourage users to observe, compare, and interpret the different configurations.

Because the system combines randomized elements with user-directed placement, no two explorations will feel exactly alike. Each universe becomes an opportunity to discover a new combination and develop a different reading. This project transforms a simple ice cream selection into an evolving map of imaginary worlds, where randomness creates the possibilities and the user helps shape their meaning.
